## Recommendation

- **NIR diffuse‑reflection** (primary): Opaque, highly scattering dairy streams are routinely monitored with NIR reflection; the technique tolerates millimetre penetration depths, uses inexpensive glass optics and standard lamps, and is listed as the work‑horse for inline dairy PAT. [S01 p.17; S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## Measurement concept

Install a stainless‑steel NIR diffuse‑reflection probe flush‑mounted in the process pipe; route the probe fibers to a bench‑top NIR spectrometer (780–2500 nm). For comparison, a second transmission probe can be mounted downstream where the product is less opaque. [S01 p.17; S01 p.5]

- **Analyzer:** bench‑top NIR spectrometer with chemometric software
- **Fibre link:** standard silica fiber (low OH) compatible with 780–2500 nm
- **Probe:** diffuse‑reflection fiber‑optic probe (scattering media design)
- **Process point:** inline, flush‑mounted at a representative point in the main line

## Spectral range

Absorption range 780–2500 nm (NIR) covering overtones of O‑H, C‑H, N‑H relevant to fat and protein. [S01 p.10]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Surface‑sensitive reflection may be affected by foam or bubbles.
- Probe fouling from fat deposits could change baseline; requires cleaning strategy.
- Temperature drift may shift spectra; need temperature compensation.

## First feasibility experiment

1. Collect representative milk samples covering the expected fat/protein range and temperature conditions.
2. Measure each sample with the proposed NIR reflection probe and with the laboratory reference (e.g., standard gravimetric fat/protein assays).
3. Build a chemometric calibration (e.g., PLS) on a training set; validate on held‑out samples.
4. If validation meets the agreed‑upon error criteria, repeat with the transmission probe on low‑fat samples for comparison.
5. Then measure in-line or in a pilot set-up against the reference method.

**Decision basis:** Success is defined by achieving comparable prediction error to the laboratory reference on independent samples; the simplest configuration (single reflection probe) is preferred unless validation fails. Agree two things separately with the customer before the test: the model accuracy required against the reference method, and the alarm or action threshold applied to the measured value.
