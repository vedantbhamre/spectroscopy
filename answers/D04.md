## Recommendation

- **NIR (transmission)** (primary): NIR transmission probes work with clear liquids, use inexpensive optics, and provide millimetre penetration depth suitable for aqueous media; cost is average and it is highlighted as ideal for online applications with standard glass optics. [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5]
- **Raman (probe)** (comparison): Raman can directly detect glucose and lactate vibrational signatures and is mentioned for metabolite monitoring in bioprocesses, but probe cost is high and signal is weak, making it a secondary option for later comparison. [S01 p.5; S01 p.16; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Increasing broth turbidity may reduce transmission path length and cause signal loss.
- Temperature fluctuations can shift baseline; need temperature compensation.
- Chemometric model may drift as cell density and media composition change.

## First feasibility experiment

1. Collect representative broth samples spanning the expected turbidity and metabolite concentrations.
2. Measure each sample with the NIR transmission probe and with the laboratory reference assay.
3. Develop PLS (or similar) calibration models for glucose and lactate.
4. Validate models on held‑out samples and on‑line runs, monitoring prediction error.
5. If transmission signal degrades, evaluate Raman probe as a backup.

**Decision basis:** Success is defined by achieving prediction errors comparable to the laboratory assay on independent validation samples; the required error threshold will be agreed with the customer before testing.
