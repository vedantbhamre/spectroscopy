## Measurement concept

Run a MIR‑transparent fiber (chalcogenide, PIR, or hollow‑waveguide) from the probe to the FTIR analyzer located in the remote building. Use chemometric calibration built on laboratory samples of oil with known water levels. [S01 p.5; S02 p.6; S02 p.13]

- **Analyzer:** Mid‑IR FTIR spectrometer with ATR input
- **Fibre link:** Chalcogenide (As‑S) or polycrystalline IR fiber, rated for the required length and temperature
- **Probe:** ATR probe with ZnSe or diamond crystal, suitable for liquids
- **Process point:** In‑line, inserted into the process pipe via a sanitary port

## Spectral range

Mid‑IR absorption range 4000–400 cm⁻¹ (2.5–25 µm) for the ATR probe; NIR fallback 780–2500 nm if needed. [S01 p.5; S01 p.10; S01 p.9]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Silica fiber will not transmit MIR light, leading to no signal.
- Long fiber runs may introduce loss; MIR fibers have higher attenuation than silica.
- High temperature/pressure may exceed fiber rating.
- Oil opacity or particulates could limit ATR penetration depth.
- The mid-IR fibre link over the required distance is not shown to be feasible in the supplied material.

## First feasibility experiment

1. Obtain a small set of oil samples with known water concentrations (lab reference).
2. Test a short length of MIR‑transparent fiber (e.g., 1 m chalcogenide) between the probe and a bench FTIR to verify transmission.
3. Measure them with a laboratory FTIR ATR bench to build a calibration model.
4. Install the probe in a pilot‑scale loop and run the fiber to the remote analyzer; collect spectra and validate against the reference method on held‑out samples.
5. Evaluate signal‑to‑noise, drift, and robustness under process temperature/pressure.

**Decision basis:** If step 3 shows acceptable transmission and step 4 validates the calibration on independent samples, the MIR‑ATR concept is feasible; otherwise, revert to NIR transmission using existing silica fiber. Agree two things separately with the customer before the test: the model accuracy required against the reference method, and the alarm or action threshold applied to the measured value.
