How to sound clear and warm

  • Read slowly. If a sentence feels too slow, you are about right.
  • Stop at every [pause] and take one breath.
  • Smile when you say the hook and the last line. People can hear it.
  • Say numbers slowly: "two point four micrometres", "ninety-seven percent".
  • If the screen shows slightly different words from this script, describe what you see. Do not read words that are not there.

1. Hook (0:00 to 0:30)

SHOW ON SCREENSAY (read this out)
  1. Home page. Keep the mouse still.
  2. On the word "SpectraSense", move the mouse slowly to Start an assessment. Do not click yet.

Imagine you work in a factory. You know what you want to measure. Maybe water in oil. Maybe the amount of medicine in a powder. [pause]

But you do not know how. Which method? Which light range? Which cable and probe? [pause]

Today, you need an expert to answer that. And experts are busy. [pause]

So we built SpectraSense. It writes a first draft of that answer in seconds. It only uses the AFS documents. And it shows the proof for every claim.

2. A hard case from the organisers: H04 (0:30 to 1:35)

SHOW ON SCREENSAY (read this out)
  1. Switch to the H04 tab (the enquiry is already in the box). Click Send.
  2. While it runs, point at the progress steps in the chat panel.
  3. When the report appears, put the mouse on the Answer: "No".
  4. Slowly move down: the premise check with the fibre names, the line about the distance, then the questions.

Let us try a hard case from the organisers. [pause]

A customer wants to measure water in a petroleum pipe. They want to use mid-infrared light. Their machine is in another building. And they want to reuse an old glass fibre cable. They ask: is this okay? [pause]

While it works, it sorts what the customer really said from what we still do not know. And it finds the right pages in the AFS documents. [pause]

Now look. The first line is the answer: No. [pause]

Why? Glass fibre only carries light up to about two point four micrometres. Mid-infrared starts at two point five. So the signal cannot get through. And it points to the AFS slide that says so. [pause]

But it does not stop at no. It lists the cables that do work: chalcogenide, polycrystalline infrared, and hollow waveguide. It says the long distance to the other building is not shown in the AFS material. And it only asks the customer for things the customer can really tell us. [pause]

This check is not the AI's opinion. It is a rule, built from the fibre ranges in the documents.

3. Proof for every claim (1:35 to 2:15)

SHOW ON SCREENSAY (read this out)
  1. Click one citation chip next to the Answer.
  2. A small box opens: document name, page number, exact sentence.
  3. Point at the line "Quote found on the cited page".
  4. Close it. Optionally click a second chip.

Every claim has one of these small tags. [pause]

Click it. You see the document, the page, and the exact sentence. And this line says the quote was found on that page. [pause]

The AI cannot make up a quote, because it picks passages by number. Every number in the report comes from the customer or from a cited page. If not, it says: "to be agreed with the customer". And a customer fact is kept only if those are the customer's own words. Anything else is marked as an assumption. [pause]

So if an expert disagrees, they do not argue with a black box. They click, read the source, and decide.

Now something it has never seen. [pause]

A customer checks water in transformer oil with a lab method called Karl Fischer titration. They want a probe in the pipe instead. And they think near-infrared needs no calibration. Right? [pause]

The assistant catches that hidden guess. The answer is no. Near-infrared needs a calibration against the lab method. The lab test stays until the new method is proven. And it cites the AFS laboratory guide. [pause]

Now look at the first experiment. It is a real plan. First, lab samples against the reference. Then a test on batches the model has not seen. Then in the pipe. And, before anyone buys anything, agree with the customer how to decide.

5. How it works inside (3:00 to 3:35)

SHOW ON SCREENSAY (read this out)
  1. Show docs/video/architecture.png full screen.
  2. Point at each box as you name it: safety gate, understand, facts and unknowns, find pages, check assumptions, write and verify.
  3. Switch to Logfire for three seconds: point at one POST /assess row and its llm assess and safety gate lines with their times. (Verified working.)

Here is how it works inside. First, a safety gate checks every message. Off-topic requests, tricks, and prompt injections are stopped before anything runs. [pause]

Then comes a chain of steps. Understand the request. Split facts from unknowns. Find the AFS pages. Check the assumptions. Write the report. And check it again. [pause]

The idea is simple: the AI writes the draft, and rules decide what stays. [pause]

And we can see every run. In Logfire we see each request, the safety gate, and every model call with how long it took, and when a backup kicks in. So if an answer is wrong, we can find out why.
